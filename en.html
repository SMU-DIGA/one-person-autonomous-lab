OPAL

One-Person · Autonomous · Lab

An accountability and evidence control plane for computational research: one person, N autonomous agents, and the infrastructure that makes research execution at scale delegable, traceable, verifiable, and accountable.

Governance is not a brake on speed. It is the precondition under which autonomous execution can be allowed to scale.

One-PersonThe unit of accountability. The system ultimately serves one person who understands the work and is willing to sign for it — not automation that leaves no one responsible.
AutonomousThe form of execution. Agents iterate freely inside an enforceable contract boundary, with no step-by-step approval; crossing the boundary halts execution rather than filing a report afterwards.
LabThe form of organization. Budget discipline, evidence chains, failure memory, and compounding assets — not a chat tool.

01

Positioning: Accountability in Computational Research Comes First

OPAL is an accountability and evidence control plane for computational research. The first release covers workflows that can be replayed from code, data snapshots, and runtime environments: model training, algorithmic experiments, simulation, data analysis, and benchmarks.

It does not try to replace scientific taste, nor does it claim that scientific truth can be derived from provenance. Its aim is to turn autonomous execution into a form of organization that a single researcher can reliably supervise: resources are capped, behavior is bounded, conclusions have sources, anomalies halt, and final accountability rests on a signature.

In Scope

Research Execution That Can Be Coded and Replayed

The full linkage between code, data, config, environment, runs, artifacts, automated gates, claims, and sign-off.

Out of First Scope

Experimental Work That Cannot Be Fully Digitized

Wet-lab, clinical, field, and hardware research can feed partial records, but “zero additional manual entry” is not a first-release promise.

OPAL does not promise to replace the researcher’s judgment of what is true. It promises that every important judgment can be interrogated, expanded, and replayed.
02

The Problem: Execution Got Cheaper, Accountability Did Not

The marginal cost of executing many kinds of computational research has fallen sharply. A single agent can run hundreds of experiments overnight, generate multiple analyses, and draft several versions. The attention of advisors, reviewers, and authors has not expanded to match.

The bottleneck therefore moves from who does the work to who reads it, who believes it, who is responsible for it. Existing quality mechanisms were designed in units of person-months. When output speed rises by an order of magnitude, they do not merely slow down — they lose coverage.

The scarcest resource is not compute. It is the number of key conclusions one person can fully understand and is willing to sign.

OPAL does not aim to make the whole cost of supervision constant. It aims to move human attention off tens of thousands of execution steps and onto the few checkpoints and material claims that genuinely need judgment.

North Star Metric
Signed claims per attention-hour
The number of material claims a single researcher can fully understand and sign per hour, at a given standard of accountability.
Optimize for accountable throughput, not for more output that nobody understands.
Costs to Drive Down
Execution-level supervision, evidence retrieval, provenance checking, reproducibility checks, and low-level quality screening.
execution supervision ≈ O(checkpoints)
scientific judgment ≈ O(material claims)

Six Layers of Responsibility: The PI’s Work, Split in Six

This is the reference frame for the system’s design. The order carries meaning: top to bottom, human involvement decreases and automation increases. Every new feature must be attributable to one of these layers, or it should not exist.

TasteLayer 1

Choosing problems, judging whether a result matters, deciding whether to publish and how to frame the story.

This is the core reason a human is in the system at all. The system supplies information; it does not replace the final judgment of value.

Human
ultimately accountable
PortfolioLayer 2

Under compute, budget, time, and attention constraints: which hypotheses to test, how much to invest, when to cut losses.

The human sets policy and constraints; the system carries out allocation, valuation, and reclaiming resources.

Human: policy
System: execution
ExecutionLayer 3

Writing code, running experiments, sweeps, ablations, debugging, reproducing baselines.

This layer will keep commoditizing. OPAL does not treat model capability itself as a moat.

Agent
EvidenceLayer 4

Linking claims back to code versions, data snapshots, configs, environments, actual runs, and artifacts.

For computational runs, provenance should be captured passively at an execution layer the researcher cannot bypass, with no extra record-keeping.

System
passive capture
Review & Sign-offLayer 5

The system presents each claim’s evidence, limitations, and automated check results; the human judges whether the method is sound, the evidence sufficient, and the conclusion important, then signs explicitly.

The system shortens the path to verification. It does not stand in for understanding.

Human
reviews, signs
MemoryLayer 6

Accumulating failures, dead ends, reusable components, anomaly patterns, and recurring risks across projects.

What makes a lab a lab, rather than a set of isolated projects, is that its failures and infrastructure compound.

System
continuous

An End-to-End Case

An abstract architecture shows where human cost is actually compressed only when it is played out on a real research workflow.

Hypothesis: adding reasoning tokens substantially raises the success rate on a class of compositional reasoning tasks, with a predictable saturation range.
01
Human

Writes the falsifier, a budget ceiling of 500 GPU-h, the minimum effect size, and the kill condition “stop after two consecutive checkpoints without improvement”, then signs the charter.

02
Agent

Inside the contract, generates 48 configurations, reproduces the baseline, and runs combinations of token budgets and seeds.

03
Gate

The leakage check blocks 3 runs. They do not enter the signable evidence set, but their full events are kept.

04
Planner

At 60% of the budget the overall effect size is below threshold; the original hypothesis triggers its kill and the remaining resources are reclaimed.

Spots, among the failed runs, a task subgroup that was not defined in advance. The system does not revive the original project; it creates a new hypothesis node awaiting approval.

05

Hypothesis Lifecycle: A Tree That Branches and Gets Cut

Research cannot be organized as a waterfall. The goal itself has to be discovered through execution, and finding halfway through that the original hypothesis is wrong is the norm, not an accident. A project charter is therefore not an executable specification. It is a living hypothesis tree.

01
Propose

Write the claim, expected evidence, falsifier, budget ceiling, and kill criteria. A hypothesis whose failure condition cannot be stated is not approved.

02
Approve

The human signs the charter, which consumes one portfolio slot and one unit of attention budget.

03
Execute

The agent iterates inside the contract. Code, data, config, environment, and artifacts land in an append-only event log.

04
Gate

Machine checks of baselines, leakage, variance, budget, and pre-registered statistical thresholds. A gate decides only conditions that can be formalized.

05
Branch

Continue, Pivot, Kill, or Escalate. Every escalation draws on its own attention budget.

06
Compile

Build a claim bundle from the evidence graph — evidence chains, limitations, failed runs, and automated check results — rather than only a fluent narrative.

07
Sign

The human judges method, strength of support, and significance, and signs the material claims.

Kill executes automatically. No override can revive the original project — it can only create a child hypothesis with new evidence, a new budget, and a new falsifier.

Chartering Is Grant-Making: An Internal Funding Mechanism

Once a hypothesis must declare its claim, expected evidence, falsifier, and budget ceiling before work starts, Propose stops being a task description and becomes a proposal. The isomorphism is structural, not rhetorical: a Planner allocating a fixed pool across a set of heterogeneous, risky applications is solving the program officer’s problem.

Grant MechanismOPAL ObjectWhere It Differs
Specific aimsclaim + scope + materialityMust be machine-evaluable, not a natural-language goal
Preliminary resultssigned evidence chains and lab-memory referencesAutomatically verifiable; no need to trust the applicant’s account
Go / no-go criteriafalsifier + kill predicateEvaluated automatically at checkpoints, not in annual review
Funding requestbudget: GPU-h, wall-clock, tokens, attentionAttention is a budget of its own, alongside compute
Stage gate / tranchestranche release + Successive HalvingBudget allocation and scheduling are the same mechanism
Track recordcalibration: predicted cost / effect size vs. actualA computable prior, not a reviewer’s impression
Final reportclaim bundle + sign-offFailed runs and exclusions are recorded by default
 Table 1 · Chartering a hypothesis is isomorphic to research funding. The value of spelling it out: failure modes already well studied in funding systems will reappear, unchanged, inside OPAL.

The price is importing the principal–agent problem into the lab. When budget must be requested, applicants have a systematic optimism bias: understating cost, overstating effect size. Agents will be worse, because winning budget is the only path to any reward — this is metric gaming upstream, at the stage where no evidence exists to check against. And proposals are cheap for an agent: it can produce a hundred a day, making approval itself the new attention bottleneck — exactly what OPAL exists to eliminate.

Tranche release

The full budget is never approved at once. One tranche is released first, and the next only when a milestone is met; this is the Successive Halving of §09 in another language.

Calibration prior

Lab memory accumulates the prediction error of each hypothesis-generation strategy. Applications from chronic over-estimators are discounted automatically, with no one having to spot them.

Scoring rule

An agent reports not “how much budget I need” but “how much uncertainty I expect to remove with budget B”. Future quota is settled afterwards against the realized information gain, which makes honest reporting the dominant strategy.

Application cost

Cap the number of concurrent application slots, or make each proposal consume a unit of attention quota. Otherwise cheap applications flood approval.

The boundary to hold: real funding systems perform worst on exactly the metric OPAL claims to optimize — slow, conservative, limited by reviewer attention, rewarding incremental safe bets. The difference is what the human does: the human does not review proposals one by one, the human writes the call — setting the funding rules (what counts as material, which risk tiers pass automatically, per-node budget ceilings). The Planner executes desk rejects and tranche releases by rule; only applications outside the rules escalate to the human. Human cost is therefore O(policy), not O(proposals).
06

Evidence Assurance: Traceable Is Not Automatically True

OPAL must state plainly what it can and cannot guarantee. An evidence system’s value comes from clear boundaries, not strong wording.

L1 · Decided by machine

Integrity

Hashes of code, data, config, and artifacts verify; history cannot be silently overwritten.

L2 · Decided by machine

Provenance

Which run, environment, model, prompt, and toolchain produced each result can be determined.

L3 · Decided by machine

Reproducibility

A third party can re-execute key runs in a specified environment, within tolerance.

L4 · Decided by machine

Validity Checks

Pre-defined baseline, leakage, variance, budget, and statistical checks pass.

L5 · Machine-assisted + human

Claim Support

Whether the evidence is sufficient to support the claim, judged by machine assistance and human review together.

L6 · Decided by human

Significance

Whether the result matters and is worth publishing. The signer bears final responsibility for this judgment.

The key distinction: reproducibility proves that the same procedure yields comparable results. It does not prove that the experimental design was sound, that the statistics were appropriate, or that the claim stayed inside its evidence.
07

Control Loops: Separate the Clock Rates, Don’t Put the Human in Every Step

Top-down goal decomposition and bottom-up fast iteration do not conflict. The answer is three control loops running at different clock rates: the outer loop issues a contract, the inner loop is autonomous inside it, and the outer loop wakes only when the contract is triggered.

Days–weeks
IntentL0 · Human

Turn taste into a hypothesis tree: claims, falsifiers, budgets, kill criteria, acceptable risk.

Here the human makes a few high-leverage writes.

Hours
PlannerL1 · System

Expand, prune, and re-price the hypothesis tree; allocate compute and attention; issue contracts; evaluate rules at checkpoints.

Event-driven, rather than constantly pushing decisions to the human.

Seconds–minutes
AgentL2 · Autonomous

edit → run → observe → repeat. Fast trial and error happens only within the action space the contract allows.

Out of bounds halts execution, enforced rather than requested in a prompt.

Supervision bandwidth comes from a structural fact: the number of human interventions tracks important checkpoints and material claims, not agent iterations.

A Hypothesis Must Be Machine-Evaluable

Hypothesis {
  id, parent
  claim        : { text, scope, materiality }
  falsifier    : failure condition, automatically or semi-automatically decidable
  budget       : { gpu_h, wall_clock, tokens, attention }
  gates        : [ baseline, leakage, variance, preregistered_tests ]
  kill         : predicate evaluated at checkpoints
  override     : may only spawn a new child node; never mutates history
  status       : proposed | running | pivoted | killed | signed
}

Contract: Not a List of Steps but a Closed Action Space

Objective

A computable metric and threshold. Not “do the experiment well”, but an explicit evaluation function and its scope.

Budget

Hard caps on GPU-h, wall-clock, tokens, and external calls. Renewing a budget is a Planner decision.

Write scope

Writable paths, network allowlist, read-only dataset mounts, and key permissions. Enforced at runtime.

Stop condition

Target met, budget exhausted, k consecutive iterations without improvement, a risk event, or a gate failure.

Artifact schema

The metrics, figures, checkpoints, seeds, failure logs, and statement of limitations that must be produced.

Escalation policy

Which anomalies need a human and which the Planner handles automatically, and how much attention budget each escalation consumes.

08

Evidence Capture, Automatic Gates, and Threat Model

For computational runs, evidence should be captured passively at an execution layer the researcher cannot bypass, rather than depending on diligent note-taking. The event log is the primary data; the evidence graph is an evolvable materialized view.

// starting point: an immutable execution identity
git      → commit sha + diff; reject unregistered working trees by policy
model    → provider / model id / version / decoding params
prompt   → content hash of system + user + tool schema
agent    → tool calls / policy decisions / intermediate artifacts

data     → content-addressed manifest; permissions and privacy policy travel with the snapshot
env      → lockfile + container digest + hardware metadata
runner   → run_id; stdout / metrics / artifact hash / resource usage
store    → append-only event log; signed checkpoints cannot be overwritten
graph    → derive claim ↔ artifact ↔ run ↔ code/data/config from the event log

Gates Check Only What a Machine Can Decide Unambiguously

Baseline

Known results, rerun, land within a pre-defined tolerance.

Leakage

Cross-checks of instances, semantic near-duplicates, and derived data; high-risk signals block automatically.

Variance

The pre-defined number of seeds, confidence intervals, and robustness reporting are met.

Selective reporting

Failed runs, discarded configurations, and post-hoc changes must enter the complete record.

Budget & policy

Crossing a resource, network, write-permission, or sensitive-data policy halts execution.

Novelty within memory

A similarity check against hypotheses already tested or killed in lab memory, to avoid unwitting repetition.

Threat 01

Metric Gaming

An agent modifies evaluation scripts, exploits flaws in the data, or optimizes a proxy metric to reach its target. Countermeasures: read-only evaluation code, an independent evaluator, held-out tests, and policy isolation.

Threat 02

Selective Reporting

Only successful runs are kept; failures, retries, and post-hoc selection are hidden. Countermeasures: every task is logged at the scheduler, and claim bundles show exclusions by default.

Threat 03

Tampered Logs and Artifacts

Outputs are overwritten or history is rewritten after the fact. Countermeasures: content addressing, an append-only log, signed checkpoints, and external timestamps.

Threat 04

Privacy and Data Exfiltration

An agent leaks sensitive data through tools or the network. Countermeasures: network allowlist, key isolation, least privilege, and a data policy recorded in the run attestation.

Threat 05

Gate Failure Itself

Automated checks are not reliable by nature. Countermeasures: versioned gates, gate test sets, independent replay, and explicit assurance boundaries.

Threat 06

Sign-off as a Formality

A signer may read only the summary without really understanding the evidence. Countermeasures: materiality tiers, mandatory spot checks, a bounded queue of open sign-offs, and recorded review time.

Threat 07

Optimism Bias at Budget Time

At application time an agent understates cost and overstates effect size to win approval, when no evidence exists yet to check against. Countermeasures: tranche release, calibration-prior discounting, and future quota settled on realized information gain.

Threat 08

Proposals Flooding Approval

Proposals are nearly free for an agent and can be produced in bulk, making approval itself the new attention bottleneck. Countermeasures: capped concurrent application slots, an attention cost per proposal, and rule-based automatic desk rejects.

09

Portfolio Planner: The Core Open Research Problem

Resource allocation cannot be reduced to ranking by estimated ROI, because the value and feasibility of a research hypothesis usually surface only after investment. Its real shape: under a bounded budget, run exploration / exploitation over a set of unknown hypotheses with mutable structure and heterogeneous reveal costs, and cut losses at the right moment.

Arms Split

A pivot spawns new sub-hypotheses, so the candidate set changes as execution proceeds. The problem is closer to dynamic decisions on a tree than to a fixed-arm bandit.

Reveal Costs Are Uneven

Some hypotheses can be killed within an hour; others need a large budget before their uncertainty falls. The objective should include information gain per unit of budget.

There Are Two Budgets

Compute and human attention are independent constraints. Every escalation, review, and sign-off must be explicitly priced.

Reward Is Not a Single Scalar

Novelty, credibility, impact, reuse value, and risk cannot be stably compressed into one score; the policy must keep the multi-objective structure and the human’s final preference.

Budget Requests Cannot Be Verified

An applicant’s cost function and probability of success cannot be verified ex ante by the principal. The core question: how to design budget elicitation so that truthful reporting is the dominant strategy (DSIC), compatible with tranche release and calibration priors.

 Table 2 · How the Planner differs from standard budget allocation problems: the open research part of OPAL.
The Planner’s academic goal is not to decide automatically what is worth researching, but — inside policy boundaries set by a human — to buy information more efficiently, expose failure sooner, and protect attention.
Starting point: Successive Halving and Hyperband can serve as the budget skeleton, but OPAL’s setting also has to handle dynamic branching, heterogeneous reveal costs, dual budget constraints, and non-stationary value functions.
A second line of research: because chartering is structurally an internal proposal (§05), the Planner is also a mechanism design problem. Budget allocation and resource scheduling meet here: Successive Halving is tranched disbursement, calibration priors are track record, and scoring rules are the incentive-compatibility condition for honest reporting.

Product: Ship the Accountability Infrastructure First, Then Attack the Planner

The first to pay are not necessarily those who most want to go faster, but those who already vouch for large volumes of automated output and for whom a single error is costly.

First Users

Leads of Computational Research Teams

Running many model experiments, evaluations, or data analyses at once, and answerable for results that are published, audited, or delivered.

Trigger Event

Agent Output Exceeds the Capacity to Check It

The number of experiments grows, but provenance, reproduction, leakage checks, and sign-off still rely on verbal syncs and hand-kept documents.

Reason to Buy

Lower Accountability Risk

Fewer unreplayable results, wrong conclusions, selective reports, and cases where “the author didn’t actually understand it”, while effective throughput rises.

Wedge

Embed in the Existing Research Stack

Connects to Git, Jupyter, Slurm / K8s, object storage, and LaTeX, with no migration to a closed workbench.

11

Open Questions

These decide whether OPAL is an internal tool, general infrastructure, or an over-designed idea. They need prototypes and real teams, not assertions.

How many material claims can one researcher reliably sign?

Supervision bandwidth cannot be measured by the number of projects alone. Materiality, a minimum standard of understanding, review time, and error rate have to be defined, and the limit measured in real workflows.

What is the minimal sufficient set for third-party attestation?

A full cluster usually cannot be replicated, and private data cannot be sent out freely. Verification levels have to distinguish bit-for-bit reproduction, statistical reproduction, trusted execution environments, and verification under restricted data.

Can the real friction of evidence capture approach zero?

Computational runs can be captured passively, but notebooks, throwaway scripts, external APIs, and non-deterministic hardware leave gaps. The product’s red line should be defined by measurable coverage.

Do customers pay for efficiency first, or for accountability risk?

The answer shapes who to sell to, the compliance requirements, and the order of the MVP. It means interviewing the people who carry signing responsibility, not only the researchers most eager to use agents.

When models begin to participate in taste, how is human responsibility redefined?

The system’s value should not rest on the premise that human taste is forever irreplaceable. The sturdier proposition: the more autonomy there is, the more behavior needs auditing, attribution, permissions, and signatures.

The goal is not to let one person produce more, but to let one person be genuinely accountable for autonomous research at scale — because it is traceable, stoppable, verifiable, and signed, it can be allowed to scale.
